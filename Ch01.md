# Chatpter 1: Understanding Large Language Models

The success behind LLMs can be attributed to (1) transformer architecture... and (2) vast amount of data on which LLMs are trained

## 1.1 What is an LLM?
* LLM is a neural network designed to understand, generate, and respond to human-like text
* Large refers to both model's size in parameters and dataset on which it's trained ; hundreds of billions of parameters, which are the adjustable weights in the network optimized during training to predict the next word
* Utilize an architecture called the transformer, which allows them to pay selective attention to different parts of input
* Can generate text so a form of generative AI (also images, media)
* ML: development of algorithms that can learn from and make predictions or decisions based on data without explicitly being programmed

## 1.2 Applications of LLMs
* Advanced capability to parse unstructured text data
* Translation, text/code generation, sentiment anlysis, summarization, knowledge retrieval from specialized technical texts

## 1.3 Stages of building and using LLMs
* Overall outcome: understand LLM mechanics and limitations; perform pre-training and finetuning
* Create custom LLMs for (1) better performance than generalized LLMs in single domain, e.g. BloombergGPT for finance, (2) confidentiality: private LLM so data not shared with third-party providers, (3) smaller size enabling local deployment directly on device, (4) ownership where updates and modifications are controlled
* "Pre" in pretraining refers to initial phase where model is trained (self-supervised) on large (trillions of words) diverse dataset of raw text (unlabeled) to develop broad understanding of language, producing a base or foundation model
* Finetuning refers to process where pretrained model is further trained on labeled dataset specific to particular task or domain
* N-shot where n is 0, one, few, n. Number of examples needed for model to perform a task
* Two popular categories of fine-tuning: (1) instruction: example query and answer pairs, (2) classification: text and class labels, e.g. "spam"

## 1.4 Introducing the transformer architecture
* Transform Architecture introduced in 2017 paper "[Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)" 
* Two submodules: encoder and decoder
* Input text -> encoder -> embedding vectors -> decoder -> output text
* Both encoder and decoder consist of many layers connected by self-attention mechanism
* Self-attention mechanism allows model to weigh importance of different words or tokens in a sequence relative to each other
** Captures long-range dependencies and contextual relationships
* BERT: bidirectional encoder representations from transformers
** Specializes in masked word prediction, so strong in text classification tasks like sentiment prediction and document categorization
* GPT: Generative Pretrained Transformers
** Designed for generative tasks, producing coherent text sequences, one word extension at a time
* Not all LLMs are transformers and vice versa
* Alternate LLMs based on recurrent and convolutional architectures; experiments to improve computational efficiency

## 1.5 Utilizing large datasets
* GPT-3 was trained on 300 billion tokens
* Meta's LLaMA expanded scope to include Arxiv research papers and StackExchange's code Q&A
* Publicly available training dataset is [Dolam: An Open Corpus of Three Trillion Tokens for LLM Pretraining Research by Soldaini et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00159)
* GPT-3 pretraining cost estimated at $4.6 million in cloud computing credits
* Use open source pretrained LLMs and openly available model weights and load them into own architecture for fine-tuning

## 1.6 A closer look at the GPT architecture
* GPT originally introduced in paper "[Improving Language Understanding by Generative Pre-Training](https://cdn.openai.com/research-covers/language-unsupervised/language_understanding_paper.pdf)" by Radford et al.
* Original ChatGPT model was InstructGPT: GPT-3 fine-tuned on a large instruction dataset outlined in "[Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)"
* Next-word prediction is a form of self-supervised learning, a form of self-labeling where we use the next word in a sentence as the label that the model is supposed to predict
** Since this next-word prediction task allows us to create the labels on the fly, it's possible to use massive unlabeled text datasets to train LLMs
* GPT architectrue is just the decoder part of the transformer architecture
* Since decoder-style models like GPT generate text by predicting text one word at a time, they are considered a type of "autoregressive" model
* Autoregressive models incorporate their previous outputs as inputs for future predictions; each new word is chosen based on preceeding sequence which improves text's coherence
* Architectures like GPT-3 are significantly larger than original transformer model (6 transformations through encoder and decoder); 96 transformer layers and 175 billion parameters total
* Emergent behavior: ability to perform tasks that the model wasn't explicitly trained to perform, e.g. models trained for next-word prediction capable of performing translation tasks

## 1.7 Building a large language model
* Three stages to build a GPT: (1) implementing LLM architecture and data preparation process, (2) pretraining an LLM to create a foundation model, (3) fine-tuning the foundation model to become a personal assistant or text classifier.
** Fine-tune with an instruction dataset to produce a personal assistant or with a dataset of class labels to produce a classifier

## Summary
* LLMs transformed the field of NLP, which previously relied on explicit rule-based systems and simpler statistical methods
* Modern LLMs are trained in two main steps: (1) Pretrained on a large unlabelled text corpus by using prediction of the next word in a sentence as a label, (2) fine-tuned on a smaller labeled target dataset to follow instructions or perform classifcation tasks
* LLMs are based on the transformer architecture and its attention mechanism of selective access to whole input sequence when generating one word at a time
* LLMs only implement decoder module, simplifying original transformer architecture which included an encoder module
* LLMs fine-tuned on custom datasets can outperform general LLMs on specific tasks
